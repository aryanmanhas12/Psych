Receipts, Not Reassurance

Every question here has a paper trail.

This screener invents nothing. Every instrument in it is a published, validated questionnaire; every design decision — screen early, track scores, share therapy tasks beyond specialists — follows peer-reviewed trials, several of them run in India. This page is the register.

Method: literature identified through the Consensus research engine (200M+ papers) and PubMed (NLM). Each entry below links to the paper. Diagnostic accuracy figures are quoted directly from the cited studies. Compiled 25 July 2026 — re-verify before formal or clinical use.

Exhibit A — Depression

PHQ-9

The nine questions map one-to-one onto the DSM criteria for major depression. They are the most-validated depression screening items in existence — including in Indian primary care, administered by non-specialist health workers.

88%SENSITIVITY & SPECIFICITY AT CUTOFF ≥10 (ORIGINAL VALIDATION, N=6,000)

The original validation

Across 6,000 primary-care and obstetrics patients, a PHQ-9 score of 10 or more detected major depression with 88% sensitivity and 88% specificity, and scores of 5/10/15/20 defined the mild/moderate/moderately-severe/severe bands this app uses.

Kroenke, Spitzer & Williams, 2001 — J Gen Intern Med [1]

44,503PARTICIPANTS IN THE LARGEST ACCURACY META-ANALYSIS

Confirmed at scale

Individual-participant-data meta-analyses in The BMJ (100 studies, 44,503 participants) confirm the ≥10 cutoff maximises combined sensitivity and specificity (0.85/0.85), which is why this app draws its clinical-cutoff line at 10.

Levis et al., 2019 [2]; Negeri et al., 2021 [3]; Costantini et al., 2020 [4]

0.92AUC WHEN ADMINISTERED BY HEALTH WORKERS IN KERALA, INDIA

Validated in India, by non-specialists

In Kerala primary care, PHQ-9 administered by public-health workers — not psychiatrists — achieved an area-under-curve of 0.92, with 82.5% sensitivity and 90.1% specificity. Hindi cultural adaptation work is also published, which anchors this project's translation roadmap.

Indu et al., 2018 — Asian J Psychiatry [5]; Bhardwaj et al., 2023 — Indian J Palliative Care [6]

Exhibit B — Anxiety

GAD-7

Seven questions developed in 2,740 primary-care patients, since tested in 24 languages across 27 countries — and one of the two most-validated anxiety tools in low- and middle-income countries.

89/82% SENSITIVITY / SPECIFICITY AT THE OPTIMAL CUT POINT (ORIGINAL STUDY)

The original validation

The GAD-7's cut point optimised sensitivity (89%) and specificity (82%), and rising scores tracked functional impairment across every domain measured. Meta-analysis pools acceptable accuracy at cutoffs 7–10, and a 2025 Cochrane review across 48 studies confirms acceptable-to-good accuracy.

Spitzer et al., 2006 — Arch Intern Med [7]; Plummer et al., 2016 [8]; Aktürk et al., 2025 — Cochrane [9]

−0.41EFFECT SIZE OF PSYCHOLOGICAL TREATMENT ON ANXIETY IN PRIMARY CARE (USPSTF REVIEW)

Why screening for anxiety is worth it

The US Preventive Services Task Force evidence review found clear evidence that anxiety treatment works — including psychological therapy alone — and that GAD-7/GAD-2 are the instruments with adequate accuracy to find the people who need it. Finding anxiety early is finding something treatable.

O'Connor et al., 2023 — JAMA [10]; Mughal et al., 2020 — LMIC validation review [11]

Exhibit C — Quick & Alcohol Screens

PHQ-4 & AUDIT-C

Triage first, full screen second — the two-stage strategy this app uses is itself evidence-based.

57%REDUCTION IN FULL-QUESTIONNAIRE BURDEN USING A 2-STAGE SCREEN (JAMA)

Ultra-brief screens earn their place

The PHQ-4 (validated in 2,149 patients) reliably flags probable depression and anxiety in under a minute. A JAMA individual-participant meta-analysis showed a two-step approach — brief screen, then full PHQ-9 — keeps sensitivity while cutting respondent burden by more than half. That's exactly this app's PHQ-4 → PHQ-9/GAD-7 flow.

Kroenke et al., 2009 — Psychosomatics [12]; Levis et al., 2020 — JAMA [13]; Arroll et al., 2010 [14]

0.891AUROC FOR DETECTING HEAVY DRINKING (AUDIT-C VALIDATION)

Three questions on alcohol, well-tested

The WHO-derived AUDIT-C detected heavy drinking with an area-under-curve of 0.891 — marginally better than the full 10-item AUDIT — making it a practical, valid primary-care screen for hazardous alcohol use, one of India's most under-recognised mental-health burdens.

Bush et al., 1998 — Arch Intern Med [15]

Exhibit D — The Follow-Up Problem

Tracking scores changes outcomes

The core complaint this project exists to answer — prescriptions without follow-up — has a tested remedy: measurement-based care, where repeated symptom scores drive treatment decisions. This app's private score history is that practice, put in the patient's own hands.

74 v 29% REMISSION: MEASUREMENT-BASED CARE VS USUAL CARE (BLIND-RATED RCT)

The landmark trial

In a randomized trial with blinded raters, depressed outpatients whose treatment was guided by repeated rating scales remitted at 73.8% versus 28.8% with clinician judgement alone — and got there in half the time. Meta-analysis across RCTs confirms measurement-based care increases remission and medication adherence.

Guo et al., 2015 — Am J Psychiatry [16]; Zhu et al., 2021 — meta-analysis [17]

2 v 4WEEKS MEDIAN TIME-TO-RESPONSE, MBC VS STANDARD CARE, IN A SOUTH-ASIAN LMIC

Proven in our part of the world

A 2025 multicentre RCT across seven Pakistani cities — a health system facing the same specialist scarcity as India — found measurement-based care halved median time to response and remission. Enhanced, measurement-driven care models show the same pattern across 29 trials and 15,255 participants.

Husain et al., 2025 — JAMA Network Open [18]; Xiao et al., 2021 — Transl Psychiatry [19]

⚖ An honest caveat: simply collecting scores isn't magic. A Cochrane review found little benefit when questionnaire results are merely fed back without acting on them [20]. The evidence favours scores that drive decisions — which is why this app pairs every result with severity-matched next steps, a fixed re-screen date, and a printable summary designed to change what happens inside the consultation.

Exhibit E — Beyond the Psychiatrist

Task-sharing works — proven in India

The manifesto's central claim — that trained non-specialists can deliver real mental healthcare — is not a hope. It carries two Lancet randomized trials from Goa.

64 v 39% REMISSION: LAY-COUNSELLOR THERAPY VS USUAL CARE (LANCET RCT, GOA)

The Healthy Activity Program

Lay counsellors delivering brief behavioural-activation therapy in Goan primary health centres nearly doubled remission from moderately-severe depression (64% vs 39%), reduced suicidal thoughts, and was cost-effective. Entry to the trial was a PHQ-9 score — the same instrument, used the same way, as in this app.

Patel et al., 2016/17 — The Lancet [21]

66 v 43% RECOVERY IN PUBLIC CLINICS: COLLABORATIVE STEPPED CARE VS USUAL CARE

The MANAS trial

Across 24 primary-care facilities in Goa, lay-counsellor-led collaborative stepped care — screen, counsel, escalate to a doctor only when needed — significantly improved recovery from common mental disorders, with the strongest effect in government facilities. India's care-cascade policy literature now builds on exactly this model, alongside Tele-MANAS and the DMHP.

Patel et al., 2010 — The Lancet [22]; Debnath, Sagar & Salve, 2025 — Indian J Psychiatry [23]
