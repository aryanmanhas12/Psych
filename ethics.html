Ethics & Privacy Charter

Promises you can check yourself.

Mental health tools ask people to disclose the things they are most afraid of. That earns an unusually high standard, so this page states what the tool does, what it refuses to do, and how to verify each claim without taking our word for it. Every claim below is checkable in your browser's developer tools or in the source code.

Part I — Data

What we collect: nothing

Not "anonymised". Not "aggregated". Nothing. There is no server to send data to — this is a static site, and every feature runs inside your browser.

You can take your data out, and you can destroy it

"My History" has an Export button that hands you your scores as a plain JSON file, an Import button to move them to another device, and a Delete all my data button that wipes them permanently.

Because nothing is ever uploaded, deletion is genuinely final — there is no backup, no copy, and no one who can restore it. Clearing your browser data also erases everything.

Verifiable: delete, then check Application → Local Storage is empty.

The shared-device problem, stated plainly

Storing data locally protects it from us, but not from someone else holding your phone. If you share a device, use a private/incognito window — your scores will not persist after you close it — or delete your history when you finish.

We would rather tell you this than let "private" imply a safety it cannot give you in every situation.

The offline cache, disclosed

Installing the app, or simply visiting it, registers a service worker that stores this site's own files — HTML, scripts, the font, the icons — in your browser's cache so everything keeps working with no connection. That is the point: someone on a patchy network should still be able to screen themselves and read a helpline number.

It caches only our own static files, never your answers or scores, and it is written to ignore cross-origin requests entirely so the live helpline directories are never served to you stale. Clearing site data removes it.

Verifiable: DevTools → Application → Cache Storage lists exactly these files. Read sw.js — it is 40 lines.

Regulatory position

Because no personal data is collected, transmitted, or processed by any operator, this tool does not act as a data controller or processor under the EU GDPR, and does not undertake data-fiduciary obligations under India's DPDP Act 2023. Data you store stays under your sole control on your own device.

If you fork and deploy this with analytics, a backend, or any transmission of answers, that changes immediately — and you become responsible for the consent, lawful-basis and security duties that follow.

Part II — Safety

Where the design refuses to be clever

Some product decisions were made by deliberately giving up capability. These are the ones that matter most.

Refused

No AI chatbot, on purpose

The "Talk it through" feature is a fixed script, not a language model. It cannot read free text and does not try to.

A generative chatbot talking unsupervised with someone in distress can miss a crisis signal, improvise clinical advice it has no standing to give, or become a substitute for the human contact that actually helps. A guided path that always ends at a validated screener, a helpline, or concrete guidance is less impressive and considerably safer.

The consequence is honest and stated in the interface itself: it will not understand you the way a person would. That is what the helplines are for, and they are one tap away from every step.

Refused

No engagement optimisation

There are no streak punishments, no notifications begging you back, no badges for daily use, no infinite feed. The daily check-in shows a streak because clinicians use symptom trends, not because we want you opening the app.

The tool's stated goal is to move you toward a human clinician and out of here. A mental health product that competes for your attention is working against its own purpose.

Enforced

The crisis path is never more than one tap away

Crisis numbers sit in a fixed bar on every page, in every language. The guided conversation carries a permanent "I need help now" button on every step, so reaching help never depends on the software correctly interpreting what you typed.

Any non-zero answer to the PHQ-9 self-harm item surfaces a crisis panel before the score is even discussed — the number can wait, that cannot.

Enforced

Screening is not diagnosis, and we never blur it

A high score means "talk to a professional." It never means "you have X." Nothing in this interface names a diagnosis for you, and every result restates the limit.

Screening instruments trade precision for reach: they are built to over-identify rather than miss people. A positive screen is a reason for a conversation, not a conclusion.

Young people

Roughly half of all mental disorders begin before age 18, so young users are the point, not an edge case. But these instruments were validated in adults, and adolescent screening carries duties adult screening does not — around consent, confidentiality, and involving a trusted adult.

If you are under 18: this tool can still help you notice something and give you words for it, but please take the result to a parent, guardian, teacher, counsellor or doctor rather than carrying it alone. Youth-specific services are listed in the helpline directories.

Part III — Limits

What we know is imperfect

Stating limitations is not a disclaimer exercise. These are real, and knowing them changes how much weight to put on a result.

Translations are working translations

The English instrument items are the original published questionnaires. The Hindi, Marathi, Bengali, Tamil and Telugu versions were produced for accessibility and have not been through formal linguistic validation and back-translation.

Officially validated translations exist for several of these instruments and should be preferred for research or formal clinical use. This is stated in-app in every language rather than hidden here.

Helpline numbers go stale

Services merge, lose funding, and change numbers. That is why the Get Help page leads with continuously maintained directories — Find A Helpline, IASP, Befrienders — and treats the country numbers underneath as convenience, with a visible review date.

An unanswered crisis number is worse than no number, so the reliable path is always shown first.

Screeners carry known bias

Instrument accuracy varies by age, gender, culture and setting; somatic items in particular behave differently across cultures. Self-report also depends on insight and honesty, both of which illness affects.

The evidence register publishes the sensitivity and specificity figures, including where they are weaker, rather than quoting only the flattering numbers.

No conflicts of interest

This project takes no funding, sells nothing, runs no advertising, has no pharmaceutical or clinic affiliation, and receives no referral fees. Nobody profits from your score. The instruments used are public-domain precisely so that access is not gated.

The code is MIT-licensed and readable in full, so these claims are auditable rather than promised.

Part IV — If You Reuse This

Conditions on forking

The licence is permissive. The ethics are not optional, and the licence file says so.

Keep the safety layer intact

If you fork, translate, or deploy this anywhere, keep the crisis helplines, the not-a-diagnosis disclaimers, and the self-harm safety trigger. They are not decoration; removing them turns a screening tool into a hazard.

If you add a backend, analytics, accounts, or any transmission of answers, you must remove the privacy claims on this page — they will no longer be true — and take on the legal duties that follow.

Deploying screening tools in a clinical service should involve a qualified mental health professional in the design. Please do that.

This document describes the software's behaviour as of the current release and is written to be independently verifiable. If you find a claim here that the code does not support, that is a bug worth reporting — please open an issue.
